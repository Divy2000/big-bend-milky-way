Come to Big Bend with me.

One of the darkest skies in the lower 48, a day's drive from where we're standing. Tents, canyons, the Rio Grande, and the Milky Way bright enough to cast a shadow.

Car, gear and power are covered. You bring a jacket and a yes.

Two ways to do it

Being honest about the sky: the moon you're looking at is full, and it decides what we'll see.

New-moon weekend

Leave Fri, Oct 9 before dawn, back Sun night, Oct 11

You getThe darkest nights of the month. Milky Way core low in the southwest after dusk, the band arching overhead until late, Andromeda visible by eye.
You give upTwelve days of waiting. In October the bright core sets by roughly 9:30 pm, so the first two hours after dark matter most.

Leave tomorrow morning, two nights in the park

You getPure spontaneity. Moonlit canyon hikes without headlamps, silver light on the Chisos, hot springs under a bright sky.
You give upThe Milky Way. The moon is still over 90% lit and up most of the night, so we'd see bright stars only.

The weekend, roughly

Built around the new-moon option. About 540 miles each way from Dallas, roughly 9 hours with stops. Most of the park has no cell signal.

  1. Friday: drive and first dark

    • Roll out around 4:30 am. I-20 west, then south through Fort Stockton and Marathon.
    • Into the park early afternoon, pay entry at the gate, set up camp.
    • Sunset at Santa Elena Canyon, then camp at Cottonwood Campground. Cottonwood Park to Cottonwood Campground, fitting.
    • Milky Way session right after twilight while the core is still up.

  2. Saturday: mountains, river, darkest night

    • Lost Mine Trail in the Chisos in the morning while it's cool.
    • Afternoon soak at the hot springs by the Rio Grande.
    • New moon night. Tripod out, lights off, stay up as long as we can.

  3. Sunday: sunrise and home

    • Sunrise over the Sierra del Carmen from Boquillas Canyon overlook.
    • Pack up by 9, back in Dallas by evening.

What we'll be looking at

Milky Way core

Low in the southwest just after dark, toward Sagittarius. Catch it early; it sets through the evening.

The band overhead

Runs through the Summer Triangle straight over camp for most of the night.

Andromeda Galaxy

A faint oval smudge in the northeast by mid-evening, 2.5 million light-years away, no telescope needed.

Photo settings

Phone: night mode on a tripod, longest exposure. Camera: widest lens, f/2.8, ISO 3200, 15 to 20 seconds.

Handled, and what to bring

Covered: the drive in the HR-V, tents and camp kitchen, and an Anker SOLIX power station with solar so phones and cameras stay charged off-grid.
